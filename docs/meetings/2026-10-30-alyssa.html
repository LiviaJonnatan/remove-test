1What MS3 was supposed to do

MS2 could only place comments that cite a section number, which is 3.7% of them. For MS3 we trained a model to link the rest: given a passage, rank the rule's sections by which one it's arguing about. Then train it on Vertex, track in W&B, and put it behind the API on Cloud Run.
Training labels come from the comments that do cite: we hide the citation and make the model recover it. No hand labelling.

Points worth making
  • Cost. The 7 test dockets have 721k passages. Sonnet on all of them is ~$14k.
  • Our confidence is useful. On our most confident 20% we're better than Sonnet (96% vs 87%); on our least confident 20% much worse (33% vs 67%). That's why sending only the unsure ones to Sonnet works.
  • New rules. Trained with no Title IX comments at all, just the rule's preamble text, we get 67% on Title IX (was 50%, hybrid search 57%). Only tested on 1 rule so far.
  • Same model size as MS2, so no new service; it just replaced the old one.
