UndersignedStrategy note · 30 Oct 2026 · AC215, Harvard

The model is a commodity. The record of what worked is not.

Today a single Fable prompt matched our custom extraction pipeline on the biggest provision of EPA's lead pipe rule. Any team with a model can read one docket. Nobody has the linked history of every comment, the agency's answer, and what changed in the final rule. That history is the product.

What we learned today

  • The prompt's quotes were 159 of 159 verbatim, with none credited to the wrong letter. Our harness added no measured accuracy.
  • Every method finds 21% to 33% of what EPA answered. The limit is the input: these runs read only letters that cite a provision by number, 41 of 1,553. Reading attachments behind cover notes (31 Oct) raises that to 74.
  • The fine-tuned section linker reached 65.5% top-1 and an autolab agent could not beat it (best 65.78% on its own split). It is frozen and serves as a cross-check.
DecisionUse the one-prompt extractor. Stop building extraction machinery. Spend the effort on what a prompt cannot do.

Risks

IncumbentsFiscalNote and Bloomberg Government sell policy tracking. Their gap is argument-level outcome data, and they could build it.
Prediction may be weakOur 11 Oct test found the comment record added little to forecasting which sections change (within-rule AUC 0.57 to 0.63). Lead with description, not forecasts.
Market sizeRulemaking tooling is a niche. This may be a strong business rather than a large one.
Data accessRegulations.gov rate limits slow corpus building. Attachments need OCR: 39 of 1,553 LCRI letters did.
