# The linker: where it stands (29 Oct 2026, evening)

Plain-language record of the trained model, for the team and the TF meeting. Every number comes from `docs/evidence/linker-train/` (generated by `python src/main.py report`), not from memory.

## The task

A comment passage goes in. The model ranks the rule's regulatory sections by which one the passage argues about. **Top-1** is the share of passages where its first pick is the section the author cited. The citation is hidden from the model, so the 3.7% of passages that cite a section give free training labels.

The test set is frozen since 11 Oct: 490 cited passages from 6 rules, from comments that share no text with any training comment, plus 40 uncited passages labelled by hand. Intervals are 95%, resampling comments.

## The model

`BAAI/bge-small-en-v1.5`, the encoder MS2 already served, fine-tuned contrastively on 4,715 cited passages: each passage is pulled toward its cited section and pushed from the wrong sections the model currently ranks highest. Same size, same serving cost as MS2. Served through ONNX on Cloud Run behind `POST /link`.

**Cascade.** The share sent to Sonnet was chosen on half the comments and scored on the other half, both ways. 71.1% is 94% of Sonnet's accuracy at 11% of its cost. The two halves picked different shares (5% and 20%), so the cutoff is not settled. For the seven dockets' 721,203 passages: about $1,500 instead of $14,121.

**Uncited passages** (n=40, one labeller): tuned 87.5% (75.0 to 97.5), Sonnet 87.5%, reranker C 75.0%, hybrid 57.5%, untuned 47.5%. The model was trained only on cited passages, so this is the check that it generalises to the 96% that cite nothing.

**Ceiling.** About 17% of citation labels cannot be recovered from the passage (11 Oct failure reading), so roughly 83% is the most any method can score this way.

## Closing the gap to Sonnet (analysis of 29 Oct)

### Where Sonnet wins

On the 232 passages both answered:

| | Sonnet right | Sonnet wrong |
|---|---|---|
| **Ours right** | 135 | 21 |
| **Ours wrong** | 40 | 36 |

Picking the right answer of the two every time would score 84.5%, so the methods make different mistakes.

| Our margin, fifth of passages | Ours top-1 | Sonnet top-1 |
|---|---|---|
| Least sure | 33% | 67% |
| 2nd | 46% | 63% |
| 3rd | 77% | 79% |
| 4th | 85% | 80% |
| Surest | 96% | 87% |

We beat Sonnet on our surest 40% and lose by 30 points on our least sure 20%, which is why the cascade works. Of all 172 test errors, 87 have the right section at rank 2 or 3, and top-3 accuracy is 82.7%, at the ~83% label ceiling. So the right answer is almost always in our top three, and the problem is ordering it.

What the errors look like (one reading by a model agent, not yet hand-checked): about 37 of 172 pick a section next to the right one in the rule, about 53 pick a section that cross-references it, and 16 pick a section no training passage ever cited (MSHA conforming amendments). Sonnet tends to win on one discriminating word ("workers" vs "students", "distribution" vs "transmission") or a function word in the section heading ("training", "recordkeeping", "reporting").

### Free signals found in the data

| Signal | Measured |
|---|---|
| The right section is cited elsewhere in the same letter | 330 of 490 test passages; in 48 of the 172 errors it is, and our wrong pick is not |
| Consecutive cited passages in a letter | Same section 46% of the time, the next section in rule order 34% |
| The proposed rule's preamble names its sections | ED 118 "Section 106.x" headings, PHMSA 77, MSHA 51, HHS 33; lead and copper and Clean Power Plan mostly topic headings |

### Ideas, ranked

Inference-only re-scoring of the served model, with weights **chosen on the test set**, so these are upper bounds until the weights are picked on validation:

| Variant | All 490 | 232 subset |
|---|---|---|
| Served model | 64.9% | 67.2% |
| + boost sections the same letter cites | 67.6% | 70.3% |
| + score against the preamble's discussion of each section | 66.3% | 69.0% |
| Both | 68.4% | 70.7% |
| Expanding acronyms from the notice | 64.5% | 66.4% |

| # | Idea | Why it should help | Cheapest test | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | **Letter context.** Boost sections the letter cites anywhere, and what neighbouring passages link to | Citations are free at ingest; +2.6 (+0.8 to +4.6) paired, measured | Pick the weight on validation, re-score. Minutes | Only helps passages in letters that cite something |
| B | **Preamble as training data.** Each preamble paragraph discussing § X is a free (argument language, X) pair: about 1,260 for ED, 570 HHS, 410 PHMSA, 200 MSHA | The agency's own discussion speaks the commenters' language. Directly targets the unseen-rule failure (ED held out: 50%) | One training run with preamble pairs, plus the ED held-out run | Paragraphs mention several sections; ACE's preamble barely names sections |
| C | **Small reranker over our top 3.** Logistic or GBM on score, margin, co-citation, preamble score, adjacency, heading-word overlap | Right answer is in the top 3 for 82.7% | Minutes on the training passages | Feature rerankers saturated before (65.9%); the gain has to come from the new signals |
| D | **Pseudo-labels for the small dockets.** Run Haiku on uncited passages; keep the ones where it agrees with us (86.5% precise on test), send disagreements to Sonnet | MSHA has 44 training passages; a few thousand labels would multiply the weak dockets | About $10 to $100 in API calls | Agreements are the easy cases |
| E | **Final rules' responses to comments.** The agency paraphrases each argument and names the section | Labels written by the agency | Download the final rules first; not verified on disk | Section numbers change between proposed and final rule; only helps transfer |
| F | **Cross-encoder on the top 3 only**, trained on our own confusions | Sibling sections differing by one word is what joint attention is for | 20 min training | The 11 Oct cross-encoder failed (48%), though with no in-domain data |
| G | **Heading windows.** A short window per section of its heading plus the preamble's subheadings | Targets the function-word errors | Inference only | Small gain, +1 to +2 |

**Ruled out by the data:** acronym expansion (−0.4), a training-frequency prior (+0.6, noise), giving the LLM the full rule text (no gain on 11 Oct), un-masking headings.

**Result (29 Oct evening):** B and A were run, with weights picked by cross-fitting rather than on test. B with A reaches 69.8% on the 232, close to the 70% hoped for; the gap to Sonnet is inside its interval. On the never-seen rule B is worth +17. The served API runs `in-pre` alone; the two added scores are not yet in `/link`.

**Run first (was):** B with A at inference. No new service, no cost per passage, and it attacks the documented weakness. If it reaches about 70% on the 232 with validation-chosen weights, the gap to Sonnet is likely inside its interval and the cascade sends fewer passages to Sonnet. Then C packages A, B and G into one model.

**Not verified:** whether Sonnet knows these real rules from pretraining (it wins some acronym-heavy footnotes), the final-rule text for E, and any retraining result. Every number above is re-scoring, not a new training run.
